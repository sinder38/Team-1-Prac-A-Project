What went well

This sprint started with planning and organising our work as a team. We discussed the Sprint Goal, confirmed everyone's roles, and reviewed the Definition of Done before beginning development.
Once the planning was complete, team members continued working on their assigned tasks and shared updates as work progressed.
Even though we faced some unexpected technical issues, the team stayed focused and worked together to find solutions. We continued updating our GitHub repository, FigJam board, and Scrum 
documentation so that the project remained organised. Overall, the team adapted well to changes and continued making progress towards the Sprint Goal.

What did not go well

The biggest challenge this sprint was the OpenRouter API issue, which caused one of the LLM models to fail because of rate limits. This interrupted part of our pipeline and meant we had to spend additional 
time testing alternative models and updating the project configuration.
Communication could also have been better. Some team members were not very active in the group chat, so it was sometimes difficult to understand the overall progress of the project or know whether someone 
needed help. Some discussions also happened through direct messages instead of the shared team channel, which meant not everyone received the same information. In addition, a few tasks depended on other work 
being completed first, which delayed progress for some members.

What we learned

This sprint reminded us that external services are not always reliable, so it is important to prepare backup solutions instead of depending on a single provider. We also learned that making the pipeline more 
fault tolerant helps prevent one technical issue from stopping the entire workflow.
Another important lesson was that regular communication is just as important as completing technical work. Sharing updates in the team channel helps everyone stay informed, makes it easier to identify blockers, 
and allows teammates to provide support when needed.

What we will improve next sprint

In the next sprint, we will encourage everyone to share regular progress updates in the group chat so the whole team has better visibility of the project's progress. We also want to identify technical risks 
earlier and prepare backup options before they affect the sprint.
Another improvement will be to spread the workload more evenly throughout the sprint instead of leaving many important tasks until the weekend. This will give us more time for testing, reviewing, and making 
improvements before the final submission. By improving both our communication and planning, we hope to work more efficiently and collaborate more effectively as a Scrum team.
