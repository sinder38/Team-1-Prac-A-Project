What went well

This sprint began with Sprint Planning, where we agreed on the Sprint Goal, confirmed team roles, and reviewed the Definition of Done before starting development. The team continued making progress on different 
parts of the project, including frontend development, backend integration, macro automation, the Delta Engine, and improvements to the prediction pipeline.
Despite several technical challenges, team members worked together to investigate problems and find solutions. We also continued updating GitHub, FigJam, and our Scrum documentation throughout the sprint, which 
helped keep the project organised.

What did not go well

The biggest challenge this sprint was the OpenRouter API rate limit, which caused one of the LLM models to fail and interrupted the pipeline. This required additional time to investigate alternative models and 
update the project configuration.
Another challenge was communication. Not everyone shared regular updates in the group chat, so it was sometimes difficult to understand the overall progress of the project or know when support was needed. Some 
discussions also happened through direct messages instead of the shared team channel, making it harder for everyone to stay informed.
The team also faced technical challenges in several areas. The Macro Agent required alternative free data sources because some original websites were paid. The frontend still needed further testing after 
connecting to the backend API, and the Delta Engine depended on the final prediction files and actual market data before calibration could be completed.
Some important work was completed close to the deadline, which reduced the time available for testing, reviewing, and integration.

What we learned

This sprint showed us that external services and third-party APIs are not always reliable, so it is important to prepare backup options instead of relying on a single provider. We also learned that building a 
more fault-tolerant pipeline helps prevent one failed component from stopping the entire workflow.
Another lesson was that regular communication is just as important as technical development. Sharing updates in one team channel makes it easier for everyone to understand the project's progress, identify  
blockers earlier, and provide support when needed.
Finally, we learned that completing work earlier in the sprint gives the team more time for testing, code reviews, and integration before the final submission.

What we will improve next sprint

For the next sprint, we will encourage everyone to share regular progress updates in the group chat so the whole team has better visibility of the project's status. We also want team members to contribute 
consistently throughout the sprint and complete their assigned tasks earlier rather than leaving important work until the end.
From a technical perspective, we will prepare backup options for external APIs, continue improving the reliability of the pipeline, and spend more time testing the integration between different project 
components.
By improving communication, planning, testing, and teamwork, we hope to make future sprints run more smoothly and deliver a more reliable project.
